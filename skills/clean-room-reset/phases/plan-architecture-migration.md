# Plan architecture migration: phase sheet

You map current reality to the approved target without letting migration convenience, enterprise habit or sunk cost redefine it. You plan; you do not edit production code.

## Inputs

The repository and its tests, the approved `TARGET_PRODUCT_BRIEF.md` when present, `ARCHITECTURE_APPETITE.md`, `CURRENT_ARCHITECTURE.md` (with its scorecard and ledgers), `CLEAN_ROOM_BRIEF.md`, `TARGET_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `~/.agents/skills/clean-room-reset/references/architecture-forces.md`.

## Method

1. **Validate the target against recovered reality.** Where the blind work missed a constraint or misjudged scale, frequency, impact or recoverability, state the missing evidence, name which stage it challenges (product direction, appetite, target, migration only) and route it back. Do not quietly add robustness to compensate.
2. **Current-to-target map** (`GAP_ANALYSIS.md`): per target behavior and concept, the current source of the equivalent, what is removed or changed, temporary compatibility required, missing boundary or ownership, current concepts to merge, inline or delete, current complexity beyond the appetite, target concepts no longer justified after seeing reality, data ownership and dependency differences, external contracts affected, and each `structural` row of the scout's scorecard with the target's answer to it.
3. **Routes.** Compare full convergence, 80/20 convergence (most ownership and maintenance value, low-value differences left), local simplification (remove abstractions, consolidate ownership, no full target) and no refactor (approved product changes and local corrections only) on customer impact, change-cost reduction, migration burden, net concept and runtime-component count, operational burden, data and rollout risk, reversibility, cost of deferral, fit to appetite. Recommend the lowest-cost route that captures the needed value. The route is the conductor's call (SKILL.md, "Founder gates").
4. **Mechanic**, preferred in order: direct deletion or in-place refactor; move and rename with stable tests; branch by abstraction only for a real dependency transition; expand and contract for schema or public-contract changes; a feature flag for a genuinely risky rollout; parallel path, shadow execution or dual writes only when evidence shows simpler migration is unsafe. Every temporary mechanism consumes the budget and has a removal slice.
5. **Characterization coverage**: approved behavior needing acceptance tests, Tier A invariants needing direct verification, temporary compatibility needing tests, superseded behavior not to canonize, Tier B repair paths needing visibility checks, unsupported cases needing boundary rejection. Test at the most stable observable boundary.
6. **Slices.** Each is labelled `STRUCTURAL`, `PRODUCT`, `COORDINATED` (only when separating would add risk) or `SIMPLIFICATION`; small enough to review as one thing, independently testable, deployable or safely mergeable, reversible where practical, an observable step toward the target, inside the appetite. Prefer negative or neutral net complexity; a positive slice cites the Tier A obligation or near-horizon use it buys. Per slice: change type, goal, target product and architecture decisions advanced, structural rows closed, complexity removed, complexity added and why simpler alternatives fail, preconditions, invariants, intentional behavior changes, implementation steps, tests and runtime checks, rollout and rollback, manual recovery where relevant, temporary structures introduced with their removal condition, the delete list, the leftover-sweep names (what the slice replaces: tables, columns, types, constants, routes, env vars, telemetry and test names, i18n keys) and the frozen names that stay on purpose, definition of done.

Every temporary structure (adapter, bridge, dual path, compatibility branch, deprecated API, duplicate model, superseded product path) has an owner, a precise removal condition, a removal slice, a usage signal and a maximum lifetime inside the migration. A temporary structure without a removal plan is new architecture.

## Output: `MIGRATION_PLAN.md`

1. Recommended route and its rationale; 2. appetite compliance; 3. the current-to-target map; 4. characterization and acceptance-test plan; 5. ordered slices with labels and net complexity; 6. data and compatibility strategy; 7. rollout, rollback and manual recovery; 8. verification matrix (every approved behavior, Tier A invariant, compatibility requirement and recovery path mapped to a method; no load, chaos or mixed-version testing beyond the envelope); 9. temporary-structure register; 10. final delete list; 11. stop-and-replan conditions: an unapproved product decision becomes necessary, evidence changes the appetite, a slice adds a runtime primitive outside the budget, a deferred case is being automated, data cannot be migrated or repaired as assumed, target ownership turns ambiguous, a dual structure cannot be observed or removed, baseline tests are unreliable.
