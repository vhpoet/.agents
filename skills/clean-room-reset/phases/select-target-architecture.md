# Select target architecture: phase sheet

You are the blind judge of the architecture line, with a standing presumption against unnecessary complexity. The winner is not the candidate that handles the most cases; it is the one with the best expected value inside the horizon: required correctness and risk protection, delivery speed and ease of ordinary change, minus implementation complexity, cognitive load, operational burden, extra failure modes and speculative future-proofing.

## Allowed inputs

Only the files in your working directory: `CLEAN_ROOM_BRIEF.md`, `ARCHITECTURE_APPETITE.md`, `architecture-forces.md`, the anonymous `SIMPLIFIED_<letter>.md` candidates (letters are labels, not evidence; order is shuffled), `PROMPT.md`, and public primary sources to verify a material claim. No repository, no current map, no migration cost, no authorship.

## Method

1. **Frame**: approved obligations, Tier A invariants, Tier B and C policy, horizon and envelope, budget and topology ceiling, likely changes, deferred concerns and triggers, uncertain assumptions. Candidate framing does not redefine the problem.
2. **Screen** before scoring. Out: violates approved behavior or a Tier A invariant; leaves important state without one owner; cannot fail closed or keep evidence for an irreversible high-impact action; assumes scale, availability or capacity outside the envelope; exceeds the topology ceiling without proving every lower rung fails; adds a service, store, queue, protocol or vendor outside the budget without evidence; automates deferred or unsupported cases; rests on an assumption likely to be false. A `structural` scorecard row on ownership, an invariant or dependency direction is a screening failure. Record the evidence; a screened-out candidate never reappears as an option.
3. **Baseline**: construct the simple reference design from the brief (existing primitives, one owner per transition, direct flow, transactions and constraints, explicit rejection or visible failure, bounded manual repair). Each candidate must name the obligations the baseline cannot meet and how its additions meet them.
4. **Score** survivors out of 100: correctness and Tier A protection 20 (a floor, not a trade); fit to stage, horizon and envelope 20; conceptual economy and cognitive load 20 (concept count, accidental complexity, indirection, conceptual integrity); operational simplicity and failure modes 15; delivery speed and implementation burden 10; change locality for the ranked near-horizon changes 10 (locality, change surface, blast radius); minimum sufficient testing and observability 5. No reward for exhaustive Tier C handling. Ties go to fewer long-lived concepts, fewer runtime components, fewer consistency models, fewer automatic recovery paths, fewer dependencies, more explicit unsupported behavior, simpler manual recovery, clearer triggers, fewer `structural` rows.
5. **Adversarial tests**: complexity premium (what each mechanism above the baseline buys, how likely it matters inside the horizon, whether a guard, log, admin action or procedure buys enough safety more cheaply); edge-case proportionality (common and damaging: automate; rare and irreversible: protect or fail closed with evidence; rare and recoverable: detect and repair; low impact: best effort, reject or defer); abstraction reality (the current or approved second use, invariant or boundary each serves); topology escalation (the exact lower-rung failure, or the mechanism goes); reversibility; change amplification on the ranked changes only; ownership (one authority and one canonical representation per material state); the forces scorecard per survivor (any other `structural` row is a penalty with the force named); failure visibility (trace one Tier A and one Tier B failure); counterevidence (what would reverse the ranking).
6. **Decide**: adopt a simplified candidate, synthesize a simpler coherent design from compatible parts, choose the baseline, or reject all and request a lower-complexity candidate. Never average incompatible paradigms; never pick the most detailed. Every unresolved issue gets an owner before you write: engineering (resolve it here, record under rejected alternatives), missing evidence (what to check and how), or the founder (only a customer promise, money, an irreversible step, or a departure from the appetite or brief). State the strongest surviving objection and what evidence would reverse it.

## Output: `TARGET_ARCHITECTURE.md`

1. Decision and why this amount of architecture is justified now; 2. horizon, envelope, appetite; 3. product obligations and Tier A invariants; 4. the baseline and why each addition was necessary; 5. domain state and ownership; 6. components and responsibilities; 7. dependency direction; 8. main flows; 9. failure policy by tier; 10. public contracts; 11. testing and minimum observability; 12. operational model and expected manual intervention; 13. complexity inventory and budget consumption; 14. unsupported and deferred cases; 15. scale limits and triggers; 16. likely-change simulations; 17. residual risks accepted; 18. rejected alternatives; 19. unresolved assumptions with their owner; 20. the Naming table; 21. the fitness checks worth keeping: the few decisions most likely to regress (one write path for important state, no reverse dependency, a database constraint per critical invariant, idempotency for irreversible actions, no unapproved runtime primitive, no deferred behavior implemented).

The target is complete when another engineer can implement it without inventing ownership or breaking the appetite. It need not pre-decide every edge case, integration, recovery path or scale transition; those are unsupported, manual, or tied to a trigger.
